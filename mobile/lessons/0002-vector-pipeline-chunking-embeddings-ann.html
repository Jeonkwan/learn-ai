Lesson 0002 • The Vector Pipeline

The Vector Pipeline: From Raw Narrative to 3ms Retrieval

How unstructured financial crime text is sliced, encoded into high-dimensional space, and queried at enterprise scale on GCP.

In our previous lesson, we established that vector search solves the fuzzy semantic retrieval problem. But how does unstructured human writing actually become something a search engine can query in 3 milliseconds?

Let's trace a concrete case through the 4 stages of the pipeline: an investigator analyzing a confidential Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) narrative.

1

Stage 1: Document Ingestion & Chunking

A typical investigation dossier spans 80 pages of text. Embedding models cannot ingest an entire 80-page book in one shot (most embedding models cap out at 512–2048 tokens). If you force it, the embedding becomes a generic blur that loses fine details.

We slice the document into discrete chunks (e.g., 500 tokens with a 50-token sliding overlap). The overlap ensures that a crucial sentence sitting right on a boundary isn't fractured.

[Chunk #042 | Source: SAR_Case_2024_09.pdf | Page: 14]
"Subject opened multiple domestic accounts at branch #104. Over a 12-day window, subject deposited structured cash sums ($9,800, $9,500, $9,200) specifically staying beneath the $10,000 Currency Transaction Report (CTR) threshold. Funds were subsequently converted into high-value antique jewelry..."
2

Stage 2: Embedding Generation (Vectorization)

We pass Chunk #042 through a neural embedding model (such as Google Vertex AI's text-embedding-004). The model projects the conceptual meaning of this text into a 768-dimensional coordinate:

Embedding = [-0.0412, 0.0891, -0.0125, 0.3120, ..., 0.0048] → (768 floating point numbers)

In this geometric space, the mathematical angle between vectors represents conceptual meaning. Even if another document uses completely different words like "smurfing micro-deposits to evade mandatory treasury filings", its vector coordinate will land right next to Chunk #042.

3

Stage 3: Vector Indexing & ANN Search (The DevOps Layer)

A bank might accumulate 10 million text chunks across past cases. When an investigator types a query:

"Find past cases where suspects used repeated cash deposits just below filing thresholds."

The query itself is converted into a 768-dimensional vector. How do we find the top 5 closest chunks among 10,000,000 candidates?

If you calculate the exact cosine similarity against every single vector (brute-force kNN), you run O(N) operations. At 10 million vectors, this takes seconds of CPU time and kills throughput.

Instead, vector databases (like Google Cloud Vertex AI Vector Search) use Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) algorithms like ScaNN or HNSW. By organizing the high-dimensional space into hierarchical clusters and navigation graphs, it finds the top 99% closest vectors in under 5 milliseconds without scanning the entire database.

4

Stage 4: Prompt Augmentation & Generation

The vector search returns the top-3 matching chunks. The application injects them into the LLM system prompt as verified context:

You are an AML Intelligence Assistant. Use ONLY the provided evidence:
[EVIDENCE 1]: Chunk #042 (SAR_Case_2024_09.pdf): "Subject deposited structured cash..."
[EVIDENCE 2]: Chunk #118 (SAR_Case_2023_11.pdf): "Evading CTR reports via smurfing..."

INVESTIGATOR QUESTION: Did we observe structuring patterns in recent watch or jewelry cases?
ASSISTANT: Yes. In SAR Case 2024-09, the subject executed structured deposits ($9.8k, $9.5k) to stay below the $10,000 CTR limit, subsequently converting the funds into antique jewelry.
DevOps Infrastructure Takeaway
On Google Cloud, you don't manage low-level vector indexing binaries yourself. You write embeddings to Cloud Storage (GCS), trigger a batch index build in Vertex AI Vector Search (powered by ScaNN), and deploy to a dedicated Index Endpoint with autoscaling.

Check Your Understanding

Retrieval Practice
In an AML pipeline indexing 10 million transactions and SAR narratives, why do vector databases use Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) indexing instead of exact K-Nearest Neighbor (kNN) search?

Primary Source Recommendation

Read Google Research's breakthrough paper behind Vertex AI Vector Search:

• Accelerating Large-Scale Inference with Anisotropic Vector Quantization (ScaNN) — Guo et al. (ICML 2020).
Focus on the introduction: it explains why standard Euclidean quantization distorts dot-product ranking, and how score-aware quantization enables Google-scale vector retrieval at millisecond latencies.
