Lesson 0008 • Vector Indexing

Scalable Vector Indexing

Overcoming the O(N) brute force bottleneck: How HNSW graphs and Google ScaNN achieve sub-5ms search.

Imagine your bank's ingestion pipeline has converted 10 million pages of financial intelligence into 768-dimensional float arrays. A compliance investigator enters a query. How do we find the top 5 closest vectors?

As a DevOps engineer, you know that calculating distance against every vector sequentially is an O(N) full table scan. At 10 million vectors, computing 10,000,000 dot products requires evaluating 7.68 billion floating-point multiplications per query! Under concurrent load, your servers would collapse.

The Paradigm Shift: Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN)

To deliver sub-5ms latencies, vector databases trade away 0.5% of absolute mathematical certainty in exchange for an algorithmic speedup of 1,000x. Instead of scanning everything, they navigate an indexed search structure.

In modern cloud systems, two dominant indexing algorithms power production vector search:

Graph-Based

HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World)

Analogy: Like a multi-lane highway express system combined with airport hub routing.

Mechanism: Builds a multi-layer graph. Top layers contain sparse "expressway" connections that span large distances across the vector space. As search gets closer to the target query, it drops down to denser local layers to zoom into the nearest neighbors.

Trade-off: Extremely high recall (>98%) and fast searches, but very memory-hungry. Storing the graph edges in RAM requires significant extra memory on top of the raw vectors.

Used in: Qdrant, Milvus, Weaviate, pgvector.

Quantization-Based

Google ScaNN (Score-Aware Quantization)

Analogy: Lossy compression tailored specifically for dot-product ranking.

Mechanism: Clusters vector space (Inverted File / IVF) and compresses 32-bit floats into compact 8-bit or 4-bit representations (Product Quantization). Crucially, Google's breakthrough (Anisotropic Quantization) ensures compression errors do not alter the top-ranked results.

Trade-off: Massive throughput, minimal RAM overhead, and ultra-fast hardware vector acceleration (using AVX-512 and Google TPU/GPU instructions).

Used in: Google Cloud Vertex AI Vector Search.

Memory Footprint Calculation for DevOps

When sizing cloud nodes for a 10-million vector index (768 dimensions):

  • Raw Float32 vectors: 10M × 768 dimensions × 4 bytes = ~30.7 GB of pure vector data.
  • HNSW Graph Overhead: Adds roughly 1.5x–2x memory for edge pointers → ~45 GB to 60 GB RAM required in memory.
  • ScaNN Quantized Index: Compresses vectors to 8-bit → reduces footprint to ~8 GB to 12 GB, drastically lowering VM hosting costs.
Architectural Insight
This is why Google powers Vertex AI Vector Search with ScaNN: it allows indexing billions of items at fractions of the hardware cost required by uncompressed graph indexes.
