Lesson 0018 • GraphRAG Architecture

The GraphRAG Paradigm: Local vs. Global Retrieval

Why traditional Vector RAG fails on corpus-wide analytical questions, and how GraphRAG community summaries solve it.

In standard vector RAG, search is fundamentally Local: an investigator asks a question, and the engine retrieves 3 or 5 specific chunks that talk about that exact topic. This works brilliantly for targeted questions: "What did Subject X deposit on Tuesday?"

Now consider what happens when the Chief Risk Officer of your bank asks a Global question:

"What are the top 3 most prevalent money laundering typologies operating across our entire archive of 50,000 cases?"

Why Vector RAG Fails Completely on Global Questions

When an LLM searches for "top 3 most prevalent money laundering typologies" in a vector database, it looks for chunks that literally discuss that topic. But no single investigator wrote a memo stating the answer—the answer is an emergent macro pattern distributed across thousands of separate files.

Vector RAG returns 5 random chunks, hallucinating or presenting a tiny, biased fraction of the truth.

The GraphRAG Breakthrough (Microsoft Research)

In 2024, Microsoft Research introduced GraphRAG to solve this fundamental limitation through pre-computed community summarization:

1. Local GraphRAG (Entity-Centric)

Query Type: Focused, exploratory questions about specific targets.

Example: "Who controls BlueSky Trading and what accounts received its funds?"

Mechanism: Uses vector search to find the "anchor" entity in the graph, then expands 1 to 2 hops along edges to fetch connected entities and transaction narratives.

2. Global GraphRAG (Corpus-Centric)

Query Type: High-level synthesis, thematic analysis, and broad audits.

Example: "What corporate jurisdictions are most heavily used to evade sanctions across our portfolio?"

Mechanism: Hierarchical community summaries (generated by Leiden clustering). The LLM reads high-level community reports instead of raw individual chunks.

How Global Community Reports Work

  1. Extract Graph: All 50,000 filings are converted into entities and relationships.
  2. Hierarchical Clustering: Algorithms group entities into communities (e.g., Level 0: 5,000 small clusters; Level 1: 500 regional sectors; Level 2: 20 macro syndicates).
  3. Pre-Generate Reports: An LLM generates an executive summary report for each community in advance.
  4. Map-Reduce Synthesis: When an executive asks a global question, the system queries the community reports in parallel and synthesizes a comprehensive final answer.
