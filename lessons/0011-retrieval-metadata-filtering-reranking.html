Retrieval, Metadata Filtering & Cross-Encoder Re-Ranking

The two-stage retrieval funnel: Sifting 10 million chunks down to the 3 gold facts.

A naive RAG pipeline takes a query, runs vector search to grab the top 5 chunks, and dumps them straight into the LLM prompt. In real-world financial intelligence, this simple approach fails regularly.

Why? Because vector embeddings compress 500 words of nuance into a single 768-dimensional coordinate. It captures general topical similarity, but it frequently brings back chunks that are topically adjacent but factually irrelevant. Furthermore, it struggles with exact identifiers like SWIFT codes or passport numbers.

The Production Two-Stage Retrieval Funnel

Enterprise search systems use a multi-stage funnel that balances computational speed against deep linguistic precision:

1. Metadata Pre-Filtering with Vertex AI Restricts

Never do "post-filtering" (fetching 10 vectors and throwing away 8 because they are from the wrong country). You might end up with zero results!

In Vertex AI Vector Search, you use Restricts. During indexing, you attach allow/deny tokens to the JSONL record:

{
  "id": "chunk_9901",
  "embedding": [...],
  "restricts": [
    { "namespace": "jurisdiction", "allow": ["CY", "EU"] },
    { "namespace": "case_status", "allow": ["OPEN"] }
  ]
}

At query time, the ScaNN engine restricts its proximity graph traversal exclusively to nodes matching those namespace tokens.

2. Hybrid Search (Dense Vectors + BM25 Keywords)

  • Dense Vectors: Excellent at conceptual synonyms ("cash smurfing" ↔ "structuring below thresholds"). Terrible at exact strings ("IBAN GB29XIB1002").
  • BM25 (Sparse Keyword): Inverted text index. Perfect at finding exact account numbers, tax IDs, and foreign names.
  • Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF): Merges the ranked lists from both search engines into a balanced top-50 candidate set.

3. The Stage 2 Re-Ranker (Cross-Encoder)

An embedding model is a Bi-Encoder: it encodes the query and the chunk separately, without them ever interacting. This makes it lightning-fast for indexing, but limits accuracy.

A Cross-Encoder Re-Ranker feeds both the query and the candidate chunk into a transformer model together. The model's attention mechanism directly compares every word in the query against every word in the document, calculating an exact relevance score (0.0 to 1.0). Running a cross-encoder on 10 million documents would be impossibly slow; running it on the top 50 candidates takes just 40ms.
